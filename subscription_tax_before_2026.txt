SUBSCRIPTION TAX BEFORE JANUARY 2026
How the dashboard fills in the line the Summary sheets never had
Written 30 Jul 2026, updated the same day when the EU export arrived


WHAT WAS MISSING

The workbooks only report subscription tax as its own row from January 2026, where
the Summary splits the subscription block into "- Line Item Total" and "- Taxes".
Before that there is no such row anywhere in the workbook.

Everything else was always tracked. Subscription revenue has its own line for the
whole history. Subscription tax is the single exception.


THE MONEY WAS NEVER MISSING, ONLY THE SPLIT

Before 2026 the region block's "- Taxes" row is the FULL tax, subscriptions
included. This is verifiable rather than assumed:

  - For every month from Oct 2024 to Dec 2025, that row equals the DTC P&L's own
    total tax row to the cent.
  - From Jan 2026 onwards the two rows (product tax + subscription tax) add back
    to that same P&L total.

So DTC Revenue was always complete. What was wrong was the split: the product tax
line was overstated by the subscription tax, and therefore so was the
"excl. renewals" headline, which is the product side only. That made 2025 look
better than 2026 for no reason other than bookkeeping.


HOW THE PRE-2026 FIGURE IS DERIVED

US - measured, not estimated.
  Taken from the Metorik export (App_py/data/renewals_daily_us.csv, produced by
  scripts/build_renewals_series.py). Metorik reports each day twice, with and
  without renewals; the tax difference between the two is the subscription tax.

  Validated against the workbook across the seven months of 2026 where both exist:

      workbook  207,619
      Metorik   207,436     off by -0.09%

  Monthly gaps were -149, -15, -64, -212, -113, 0 and +370. The one visible
  outlier is July 2026, a part month.

EU - measured too, since 30 Jul 2026, when the EU export arrived.
  The export reports in euros, while the workbook reports dollars converted at the
  daily rate, so no euro amount in it can be used as it stands. A ratio, though, is
  the same number in either currency. So what is taken from it is the day's renewal
  SHARE of tax, applied to the workbook's own dollar tax line.

  Validated the same way as the US, across the seven months of 2026 where the
  workbook reports EU subscription tax itself:

      workbook  31,392
      Metorik   31,411     off by +0.06%

  Four of the seven months come out exact to the cent; the widest miss is 0.96%.
  The 21% VAT model it replaced was off by -1.69% over the same months, so this is
  the better of the two, though both were close enough that the pre-2026 EU figure
  barely moved: 31,356 measured against 31,200 modelled, on a combined
  reclassification of 274,689.

  The rate is still there as a fallback for the months before EU renewals begin,
  where a renewal share of zero would otherwise miss the tax on brand-new
  subscriptions. That covers Oct and Nov 2024.


WHAT IT CHANGES

The amount MOVES between two rows. Nothing is added and nothing is removed, so
every "incl. renewals" total is untouched:

    FY2024   32,277,844.42     unchanged
    FY2025   41,334,295.20     unchanged
    Jul 2026    621,881.53     unchanged

The "excl. renewals" figure changes, because the subscription tax now correctly
falls outside it:

    FY2024   30,949,167   ->   30,897,774.85    (moved  51,392)
    FY2025   34,681,060   ->   34,457,762.65    (moved 223,297)
    Jul 2026    278,855.73 ->     278,855.73    (unchanged)

2026 does not move because the workbook already split it there. The earlier years
were brought onto 2026's basis, not the other way round, so year-over-year is now
like-for-like.


SAFEGUARDS

  - The carve-out is capped at whatever the tax line actually holds, so it can
    never push a tax figure negative.
  - Checked on all 731 pre-2026 days: no day needed capping, and the tightest
    margin was still 171.61 clear.
  - US + EU equals the combined figure exactly for both tax columns.
  - The implied US rate lands at 3-4% from Dec 2024 onwards, matching 2026's
    3.3-4.1%. Early 2024 looks erratic in percentage terms, but on a subscription
    base of a few thousand dollars the amounts are two and three digits, so it
    makes no material difference.


WHERE IT LIVES

  App_py/app.py, function split_subscription_tax()
    SUBSCRIPTION_TAX_SPLIT_FROM = 2026-01-01   (workbook starts splitting here)
    EU_SUBSCRIPTION_TAX_RATE    = 0.21         (fallback only, pre-renewal months)

  App_py/data/renewals_daily_us.csv    daily, amounts and counts
  App_py/data/renewals_daily_eu.csv    daily, shares and counts only
    Both written by scripts/build_renewals_series.py from the exports in "wo
    renewals/". Rerun that script whenever a new export lands.

It runs at load time, not in the sync, so App_py/data/revenue_data.csv stays a
faithful copy of what the workbooks say and the derived part sits in one place. It
is applied before the region columns are computed, so US, EU and combined all come
out consistent.
